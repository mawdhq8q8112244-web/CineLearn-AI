A comparative analytical study of generative AI-based tools and systems in education, identifying the gap toward a learning system grounded in narrative, generation, and adaptive assessment.


introduction

In recent years, the education sector has undergone an accelerated transformation driven by advancements in artificial intelligence—specifically, Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI). These technologies are now capable of producing text, images, audio, and video, as well as analyzing vast amounts of educational content and reconfiguring it in various ways.
This development has given rise to a new trend in educational technology that goes beyond merely delivering information to students; it seeks to fundamentally reshape how that information is presented. Rather than remaining confined to traditional formats—such as lectures, PDF files, or slide presentations—educational content can now be transformed into videos, interactive conversations, learning activities, assessments, or personalized learning experiences tailored to the individual needs of the learner.
Recent studies indicate that the use of AI-generated educational videos has become an emerging area of ​​research, particularly within higher education. A review of research published since 2023 reveals that AI-driven video production systems generally fall into two categories: fully AI-generated content and AI-assisted production. These studies highlight potential benefits in areas such as efficiency, scalability, content accessibility, personalization, learner engagement, and information retention. At the same time, the literature emphasizes that the true value of these technologies lies not merely in their capacity to generate video, but in how effectively they are integrated into instructional design, interactive processes, and assessment strategies. In parallel with developments in video production, another trend has emerged: personalized and adaptive learning. These systems utilize learner performance data to adjust content, difficulty levels, or learning pathways. Recent systematic reviews indicate that AI-powered adaptive learning systems now leverage technologies such as machine learning, deep learning, natural language processing, and multimodal analytics to craft educational experiences tailored to the specific characteristics and performance of individual learners.
Consequently, the educational landscape is shaped by several concurrent trends—ranging from the analysis of source materials and video production to learner interaction, assessment, and adaptive feedback. It is therefore essential to examine existing systems before proposing a new one; determining the scholarly or practical value of a project requires a clear understanding of what has already been developed, the capabilities of current systems, and the limitations of their functionality.
This study analyzes five tools and systems—NotebookLM, HeyGen, OpenMAIC, TutorFlow, and MimWise—that are either directly or partially relevant to the proposed project. These systems were selected because they represent distinct trends within the ecosystem of AI in education, spanning knowledge and source analysis, video and visual content production, and learner interaction, assessment, and review. The analysis aims to define the nature, scope, and operational mechanisms of each system, subsequently comparing their functions against the proposed project to identify the specific gap that the new initiative seeks to address.


Generative Artificial Intelligence and the Reshaping of Educational Content

In its early stages, the use of artificial intelligence in education was primarily associated with "Intelligent Tutoring Systems" and the automated analysis of student performance. Recent advancements in generative AI, however, have expanded this scope to include the creation of the educational content itself.
Systems are no longer limited to simply answering a student’s question; they are now capable of analyzing an entire lecture, extracting key concepts, rephrasing them, generating examples and questions, creating images or videos, and even producing virtual characters that can participate in the instructional process.
This shift is significant for the proposed project, as the concept goes beyond merely creating a new video tool; it seeks to leverage generative capabilities to re-represent educational knowledge in a novel way. The idea is to transform educational material into a script—essentially a story or a short educational film—where scientific concepts, terminology, and relationships become integral parts of a narrative world.
Nevertheless, producing engaging content alone does not constitute a complete learning system. Recent research on "personalized learning" emphasizes that true personalization requires utilizing data on the learner and their performance, rather than simply altering the format of the content. Studies have shown that feedback and adaptation to a learner’s performance are core components of AI-driven personalized learning systems.
Consequently, the proposed project consists of two interconnected levels: a "Generative Learning Experience" level, which presents the material through narrative and visual means, and an "Assessment and Adaptation" level, which uses student outcomes to verify that learning has occurred and to identify areas that may require further review.


Analysis of existing systems and tools

NotebookLM

NotebookLM represents an approach that anchors artificial intelligence to user-provided sources—a concept known as "source grounding," where original materials become an integral part of the context within which the system operates.
The platform allows users to incorporate various types of sources, which can then be analyzed and interacted with to generate outputs that facilitate understanding and study. The capabilities of NotebookLM have expanded to include "Video Overviews," and in 2026, Google introduced a more advanced feature called "Cinematic Video Overviews," capable of creating richer visual content based on the sources supplied by the user.
For the proposed project, the significance of NotebookLM lies in how it handles original source material. The project requires a mechanism that ensures the resulting narrative remains strictly tethered to the academic content; for instance, if a student inputs a lecture, the characters, events, and information depicted in the film must be derived directly from the concepts presented in that lecture.
In this sense, source grounding serves as a foundational principle that can be leveraged for the proposed system, though the nature of the experience differs. While NotebookLM focuses on comprehending sources and re-presenting them in various formats, the proposed project aims to transform knowledge into a continuous narrative experience—where academic content is woven into the very structure of the story—and subsequently link this experience to an assessment process for the learner.
This distinction has become even more critical now that NotebookLM itself is capable of generating "Video Overviews," meaning that the mere production of AI-generated video can no longer be considered a unique gap in existing tools. Consequently, the distinct value of the proposed project must reside in the nature of the overall educational experience, rather than simply in the inclusion of AI-generated video.


 HeyGen


HeyGen represents another trend in AI video generation, focusing on transforming text-based content into video through the use of virtual avatars, synthesized speech, and visual elements.
Such systems are significant in education, as they reduce the need for traditional filming processes and enable the faster production of educational content. HeyGen has, in fact, appeared among the tools utilized in studies exploring AI-generated educational videos.
However, while the primary function of this system is video production, video itself is not the ultimate goal of the proposed project. Instead, the video serves as one stage within the broader educational process—preceding assessment, the analysis of student performance, and the identification of concepts that require further review.
Furthermore, the type of video proposed here differs from the conventional model, which typically features a presenter or an avatar explaining information. The proposed approach involves transforming the concepts themselves into elements within a narrative: a scientific concept might be represented as a character, a relationship between two concepts might manifest as an event within the story, or the visual environment might be used to embody an idea that is difficult to explain through text alone.
Consequently, while technologies such as AI avatars, text-to-video generation, and AI-driven narration contribute to the production aspect of the system, its core educational value remains rooted in narrative design and subsequent assessment.



OpenMAIC

OpenMAIC represents a more advanced trend in the field of Interactive AI Learning. Developed by the THU-MAIC team at Tsinghua University, this open-source system aims to transform a given subject or document into an interactive learning experience powered by multi-agent systems.
The system employs a collection of intelligent agents—such as a virtual instructor and virtual peers—to create a learning environment that mirrors an interactive classroom. The platform offers lessons, slides, quizzes, simulations, discussions, and direct interaction between the learner and the agents. Its technical architecture relies on LangGraph to manage state transitions and interactions among the agents.
The significance of OpenMAIC lies in its demonstration that educational AI can evolve from a static content model toward an interactive environmental model. Beyond merely generating lessons, the system incorporates quizzes, assessments, feedback, and the identification of knowledge gaps.
Consequently, OpenMAIC stands as one of the systems most closely aligned with certain future-oriented aspects of the proposed project. However, a key distinction lies in the nature of the learning environment: while OpenMAIC constructs a multi-agent "classroom environment," the proposed project is built upon a "narrative environment"—a story-driven world where concepts, characters, and events are integral to the learning experience.
Thus, OpenMAIC can be viewed as a valuable model for concepts related to interaction and assessment, rather than an exact template for the proposed project.


TutorFlow


TutorFlow focuses on producing educational videos using artificial intelligence, enabling the transformation of a topic or a brief idea into an instructional video that incorporates text, voiceover, scenes, and visual elements. The platform is designed for creating recurring video content for courses and training, offering features such as AI characters, narration, and subtitles, with the ability to generate videos in various formats suitable for both formal instruction and short-form content.
TutorFlow serves as a clear example of the shift from traditional instructional content creation to AI-assisted video production; in this sense, it aligns closely with the aspect of producing educational episodes as envisioned in the proposed project.
However, a fundamental difference lies in the ultimate objective. While TutorFlow focuses on producing a finished, ready-to-use educational video, the proposed project aims to build an educational experience that extends well beyond the video itself. In this model, the video is not the end point, but rather one component of a learning cycle that encompasses viewing, assessment, performance analysis, and review.
Furthermore, the proposed project does not merely aim to produce conventional visual explanations; instead, it seeks to construct a "narrative learning experience" by transforming concepts into interconnected characters, elements, and events within a cohesive story.


MimWise


MimWise addresses a specific challenge: understanding and reviewing lecture material. The platform functions as an AI tutor for lectures and presentation slides, capable of transforming educational PDFs into guided explanations, reviews, practice exercises, and interactive quizzes. It also incorporates a narrative, interactive approach within certain educational review modes.
The significance of MimWise lies in its alignment with the proposed concept of integrating educational content with interaction and practice. However, while MimWise is primarily centered on lectures, reviews, and practice exercises, the proposed project focuses on developing a complete narrative film or episode based on the educational material, followed by post-viewing assessments to gauge the level of comprehension.
In this sense, MimWise offers a valuable model for the idea that visual or narrative-driven content can be effectively paired with practice and assessment—a concept that can be leveraged when designing the evaluation phase of the proposed project.


From content production to building a learning course


An analysis of existing tools reveals that the tech market is not lacking in solutions for educational content production. There are systems capable of analyzing source materials, generating videos, building interactive classrooms, and assisting with lecture review.
However, the distinctions between these systems become more apparent when one considers the educational process in its entirety.
For instance, a tool like NotebookLM addresses challenges related to accessing knowledge and comprehending source materials; HeyGen focuses on video production; TutorFlow facilitates the rapid creation of educational videos; OpenMAIC handles multi-agent interaction and assessment within an educational environment; and MimWise centers on explaining and reviewing lectures.
Consequently, the research question for this project is not: "Can educational videos be generated using artificial intelligence?"
The answer to that is already well-established.
The more significant question is: Can the generation of narrative and visual content be integrated into an educational process that is both measurable and adaptive—one that guides the student from content engagement to assessment, and subsequently to review, based on their individual performance?
This is where the project’s proposed contribution lies.


Evaluation as an essential component of the proposed system


The proposed project does not rely on the act of watching a film as evidence of student learning. While watching may make the material more engaging, it does not guarantee that the student has grasped the concepts or is able to apply them correctly.
Therefore, the project proposes the inclusion of an "experimental quiz" following each educational episode. This quiz is designed to measure the student's comprehension of the concepts presented within the narrative.
The system’s role extends beyond merely assigning a final score; the quiz serves as a source of learning data that can be analyzed. If a student repeatedly answers questions related to a specific concept incorrectly, the system can identify this as a "knowledge gap" that requires further review.
In this way, the system moves beyond simple content delivery toward "learner modeling"—developing an understanding of the student’s proficiency level based on their performance.
This approach aligns with contemporary trends in "adaptive learning," where systems utilize learner performance data to adjust content, sequence activities, or provide feedback. Recent reviews indicate that adaptive AI-driven systems employ various techniques to analyze learner performance and construct personalized learning pathways.
Consequently, the system could evolve in the future to go beyond just reporting a quiz score; it could identify specific concepts requiring review, re-present the relevant theoretical material, and then allow the student to retake the quiz.
This aspect is crucial to defining the nature of the project: the system is not merely a tool for "entertainment-based learning," but rather a model that integrates engagement, assessment, and adaptive learning.


The Proposed Educational Model


The proposed system begins with educational material provided by the user—such as a lecture, a PDF file, a text, or other study resources. The system analyzes this content to extract key concepts, terminology, and relationships, subsequently using this information to construct an educational scenario.
This scenario is not a literal transcription of the original material; rather, the knowledge is reorganized into a narrative structure that helps represent concepts more clearly. For instance, specific terms may be personified as characters, relationships between concepts may be depicted as events, and the visual environment may be used to embody abstract ideas.
Once the scenario is structured, the visual content generation phase begins. The final output can take the form of an animation, an AI-generated video, or a blend of virtual characters and generated scenes, depending on the technical capabilities available during the project's implementation.
It is important to distinguish between the core elements of the project and areas reserved for future development. The primary objective is to demonstrate the feasibility of transforming educational content into a narrative experience that can be viewed and evaluated. Conversely, producing full-length, realistic films, developing highly sophisticated characters, or implementing a complete cinematic production system—should these exceed the resources available for the graduation project—can be considered objectives for future development.
Following the educational segment, the student proceeds to an assessment. The results of this assessment are used to analyze the student's performance and identify concepts that require further review.
In an advanced version of the system, the process could transition from assessment to targeted theoretical review, followed by a re-assessment—thereby establishing a comprehensive, cyclical learning process.


Data and Content Sources


The source of data is a fundamental question that may arise during the discussion of a project, particularly in systems based on generative artificial intelligence.
A project does not necessarily need to build a massive generative model from scratch, nor does it require collecting millions of films and scripts to train a custom model. Such an undertaking would be beyond the scope of a graduation project with limited resources, and it is not essential for demonstrating the core concept.
The system can be built using educational materials—provided by the user—as the primary source of content. In this approach, lectures, books, educational files, and texts serve as the data to be analyzed; the system then utilizes existing generative AI models to transform this knowledge into scripts and scenes.
As for incorporating cinematic styles or characters inspired by popular films, this can be handled at the level of stylistic approach or narrative genre, rather than by copying protected characters or reproducing content from the original films. Furthermore, the system could allow users to suggest a story concept, setting, or character archetype, which the AI ​​would then use to craft an original educational narrative based on the study material.
In this framework, the educational content serves as the system's foundational data, while the generative output is produced by processing this material through AI models.
Moreover, for the purpose of a graduation project, there is no need to train a custom video-generation model; instead, one can leverage existing generative models and services. The innovation, therefore, lies in the system layer that bridges the analysis of educational material with narrative development, content generation, and evaluation.

Research gap

The study indicates that the existing gap is not a lack of tools for producing educational videos, nor a shortage of instruments for assessment or interactive learning; such functionalities are already available—either as standalone tools or within specialized systems.
Furthermore, claiming that a project is innovative simply because it can generate videos using artificial intelligence is no longer accurate, as current research and existing tools have already demonstrated this capability. A recent review shows that platforms like HeyGen have already established themselves within the domain of "AI-Generated Instructional Videos."
Consequently, the identified gap lies in the level of integration between these various functions.
The proposed system does not treat a video as a final, isolated product; rather, it views it as one component of a broader learning experience. This experience encompasses the educational material, knowledge analysis, narrative construction, visual content generation, student assessment, performance analysis, and the identification of concepts requiring review—with the potential for future iterations, such as re-presenting content and re-assessing the learner.
Thus, the research value lies in the synergy between Generative AI, Narrative Learning, Assessment, and Adaptive Learning, rather than in the use of any single technology in isolation.


Innovation in the proposed project

The innovation in this project lies in the design of the system, rather than in a claim to have invented a new artificial intelligence model.
The first level is "Narrative-Based Learning," where educational concepts and terminology are integrated into a cohesive story, moving beyond simple text-based or slide-based presentations.
The second level is "Generative Multimedia Learning," which employs generative AI technologies to create the narrative, characters, scenes, audio, and visual content based on the educational material.
The third level is "Assessment-Driven Learning"; the educational process does not conclude when the film ends—instead, the student undergoes an assessment to gauge their understanding of the material.
The fourth level is "Adaptive Learning," where assessment results are used to identify concepts requiring further review. The system can subsequently direct the student to the appropriate theoretical content and re-evaluate their understanding.
In this way, the project shifts from a model focused solely on content delivery to one based on a measurable educational cycle.


The future aspect of the project


It is not necessary to implement all advanced features from the project's initial version. Development can be structured into a foundational phase followed by future stages.
The foundational phase focuses on proof-of-concept: ingesting and analyzing educational material, creating an instructional scenario, converting it into short-form visual content, administering a test to the student, and analyzing the results.
Future stages could include enhancing video generation quality; incorporating various types of animation and AI-generated realistic video; developing interactive characters; integrating computer vision for learner interaction or the analysis of visual patterns; refining adaptive learning; and linking the system to comprehensive study paths.
Furthermore, the system could evolve from producing standalone episodes to a course-based learning model, where the curriculum consists of interconnected segments. In this model, the system would track student progress across these segments and utilize test results to determine the learner's level of mastery for each topic.
Ultimately, the project could transition from a tool for generating educational videos into a full-fledged learning platform powered by generative AI, learning analytics, and adaptive learning.


The educational value of the project


The educational value of the project stems from the integration of student engagement and learning assessment. While narrative and visual content may capture a student's attention and make the material more accessible, the true educational value emerges when the student's level of comprehension is verified.
Consequently, the assessment proposed following the film serves as a critical component of the system's design. Rather than simply viewing the content and moving on to the next topic, the student enters a phase where their understanding is measured.
Furthermore, the analysis of errors can transform test results from mere numerical scores into data that can be used to refine the educational path. This aligns with contemporary trends in AI-based personalized learning, which prioritize customization, feedback, and adaptation to the learner's performance.
In this way, the project combines motivational, educational, and analytical dimensions—a distinction that sets it apart from approaches that view artificial intelligence merely as a tool for creating more engaging videos.


The technical value of the project


From a technical standpoint, the project does not require building a new generative AI model from scratch; instead, it is possible to develop an architecture that integrates existing models and technologies into a single workflow.
The proposed architecture includes layers for processing educational material, extracting concepts, ensuring the generated content remains grounded in the source material, generating scenarios, producing scenes, testing, and analyzing learner performance.
This approach is more realistic for a graduation project, as it leverages available generative models rather than attempting to train a massive video model—a task that would demand extensive computational resources and vast amounts of data.
Furthermore, this modular architecture allows each component to be developed independently. For instance, the video generation model or the language model could be replaced in the future without the need to rebuild the entire system.


Challenges and Limitations


Despite the project's significant potential, its development faces several scientific and technical challenges. A primary concern is maintaining scientific accuracy when transforming subject matter into a narrative; altering the phrasing of concepts to make them more engaging risks compromising their original scientific meaning.
Furthermore, ensuring consistency across characters and scenes presents a challenge in generative video production, particularly when an episode consists of numerous distinct scenes. Additional hurdles include the operational costs of generative models and the time required for video production.
There are also challenges related to privacy and student data—especially if test results or other personal information are stored. Recent reviews in the field of adaptive learning highlight privacy, scalability, and the interpretability of model-driven decisions as critical challenges for AI-based educational systems.
Finally, the use of realistic characters or images of users necessitates clear policies regarding consent and privacy. Consequently, such features are better suited for future development phases rather than being included as core requirements in the initial version.


Discussion


A comparison reveals that the current evolution of educational artificial intelligence is not trending toward a single, all-encompassing tool, but rather toward a suite of specialized systems. Consequently, developing a new project is less about adding an isolated technology and more about how these various functions are integrated.
Tools like NotebookLM have demonstrated the value of linking AI with educational resources; platforms such as HeyGen and TutorFlow have shown how AI can be applied to video production; OpenMAIC offers an advanced model for interaction and assessment through intelligent agents; and MimWise provides a framework that combines lecture explanation, review, and practice.
Collectively, these systems suggest that the core challenge is no longer the AI’s ability to generate content, but rather the design of an educational experience that effectively leverages such content.
Accordingly, the proposed project does not claim that its individual components are unique to the market. Instead, it focuses on integrating these elements into a model centered on "narrative learning," while linking the viewing experience to processes of assessment and adaptation.
This type of innovation represents a redesign of how existing technologies are applied to address an educational challenge—an approach that is far more realistic and feasible than attempting to develop a new video-generation model from scratch.


Conclusion

The study demonstrates that Generative AI technologies offer a broad spectrum of educational capabilities—ranging from source analysis and knowledge comprehension to the creation of videos, characters, and interactive content, as well as assessment and adaptive learning.
An examination of the five systems reveals that each has a distinct focus: NotebookLM centers on source-grounded learning; HeyGen on AI video generation; OpenMAIC on multi-agent interactive learning; TutorFlow on AI-assisted educational video creation; and MimWise on lecture understanding and revision.
The value of the proposed project does not lie in claiming the novelty of these technologies themselves, but rather in reconfiguring them into a unique learning experience. This approach transforms educational material into a narrative or short film, where concepts and terminology are embedded within a story-driven world, followed by a post-viewing assessment to gauge student comprehension.
Analyzing the results of these assessments is a core component of the proposed system. It paves the way for evolving beyond the mere production of engaging content toward "adaptive learning"—leveraging learner data to identify specific areas of weakness and guiding students toward targeted review.
In essence, the project can be defined as a Generative AI-based educational system that aims not only to alter the presentation of educational material but to reconstruct the learning cycle itself by integrating narrative-based learning, generative multimedia, assessment, learning analytics, and adaptive learning.
The actual scope of implementation will be determined by the resources available during the graduation project. Consequently, the core functionalities will be developed first, while more advanced features—such as high-quality realistic video, sophisticated characters, multi-agent interaction, and computer vision—may be reserved for future development phases, contingent upon the outcomes of the initial implementation.
