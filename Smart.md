### Studying and applying the SMART methodology to project objectives


## introduction


During the project planning phase, it was essential to identify a methodology that would help define and organize objectives clearly—one that would not only describe what we intend to implement but also outline how these objectives can be achieved, how their success can be measured, and how to ensure they are feasible within the available resources and timeframe.
Upon reviewing the SMART framework, I found it well-suited for organizing project objectives, as it relies on five criteria that help formulate goals with greater precision and realism.
Consequently, I adopted this methodology to examine and review the project’s objectives, ensuring that each one contributes to the overarching goal and that the objectives are interconnected, actionable, and measurable.



## Introduction to the SMART Methodology


SMART is a methodology for setting goals. Its name is an acronym derived from five English words, representing five essential criteria that should be considered when formulating goals:

- Specific: The goal should be clear and well-defined.
- Measurable: It should be possible to measure.
- Achievable: It should be attainable.
- Relevant: It should be aligned with the main objective.
- Time-bound: It should have a defined timeframe.

Below is an explanation of how to utilize each criterion when defining project goals.



## Studying SMART criteria and applying them to the project

# 3.1 Specific

I began applying this criterion by reviewing the sub-objectives, ensuring that each one precisely defined the required task, rather than relying on vague, general statements that failed to specify the nature of the work.
For instance, it was not sufficient for an objective to simply be "processing academic files"; it was necessary to define exactly what this process entailed—such as receiving the files, extracting text and information from them, and preparing them for analysis.
In the same manner, the remaining objectives were defined to encompass analyzing scientific content, extracting concepts and relationships, transforming knowledge into narrative elements, developing an educational scenario, producing the film, and finally, preparing assessments and evaluating student responses.
As a result, the objectives became much clearer, making it possible to precisely identify the tasks that needed to be carried out at each stage.


# 3.2 Measurable

After defining the goals, I moved on to examining how to measure the success of their implementation—since setting a goal alone is not enough unless there is a way to verify its achievement.

To this end, I established practical indicators tailored to the nature of each goal. Examples include:

- Academic file processing: Testing the receipt of various file types and verifying the successful extraction of their contents.
- Content analysis: Verifying the extraction of scientific concepts, definitions, processes, and relationships, and comparing them against the original source.
- Narrative conversion: Reviewing the resulting narrative elements to ensure they accurately represent the scientific concepts.
- Script creation: Comparing the information in the script with the academic material to ensure scientific accuracy is maintained.
- Film generation: Verifying the production of animated scenes that include appropriate characters, dialogue, and audio.
- Quiz creation: Ensuring that the questions are derived from the original academic file.
- Answer evaluation: Testing the assessment process using various responses to verify the appropriateness of the results.
  
Thus, for every objective, there is now a method to test its success, rather than simply stating that it has been completed.



# 3.3 Achievable

In this criterion, the focus was on assessing the realism of the objectives and the feasibility of achieving them using the available tools, technologies, and resources.

Given that the project relies on generative artificial intelligence, consideration was given to leveraging specialized technologies and tools to carry out its various stages, including:
File processing and text extraction tools.

-Linguistic models for content analysis and scenario creation.
-Image generation models for creating characters and scenes.
-Techniques for animating characters and visual clips.
-Voice generation and text-to-speech techniques.
-The software tools necessary to compile scenes and dialogues and produce the film.


It was also taken into account that producing an animated film—involving multiple characters, dialogue, interactions, and animated sequences—presents a technical challenge. Therefore, the execution process should be broken down into stages, appropriate tools must be selected, and a realistic scope—achievable within the project’s available resources—must be defined.
Using this criterion, one can distinguish between objectives that are feasible with current capabilities and those that might require additional time or resources.


# 3.4 Relevant

In this criterion, I focused on examining the relationship between the sub-objectives and the primary goal, ensuring that each objective serves as an essential component of the overall concept rather than merely an extraneous function that contributes nothing to its realization.

-Upon reviewing the objectives, it became clear that each stage plays a role that is interconnected with the other stages:
-Receiving academic files provides the scientific material that serves as the starting point for the work.
-Content analysis identifies the knowledge and concepts that need to be presented.
-Transforming knowledge into narrative elements links scientific information to characters and events.
-Script creation organizes these elements into a coherent educational story while maintaining scientific accuracy.
-Film generation presents the story through animated scenes, with characters engaging in dialogue and interacting with one another.
-Developing assessments provides a means to measure student understanding, based on the original source material.
-Evaluating responses helps determine the extent to which the student has grasped the content.

Through this review, it became clear that the objectives are not separate tasks; rather, they represent a series of integrated stages that serve the project’s educational purpose.



# 3.5 Time-bound

This criterion requires establishing a timeframe for achieving the project’s objectives, with time allocated across the various stages of work.

Given the nature of the project and the available completion period—approximately fifty days—following the traditional sequence of project phases was not appropriate. This is due to key technical challenges that could affect the feasibility of the concept, particularly concerning the necessary API keys and the operation of video-generation technologies.
Consequently, work began with a technical experiment focused on the most challenging component. The goal was to identify potential issues early on and verify the feasibility of achieving viable results, all under academic guidance and supervision.

Based on this, a preliminary timeline can be outlined as follows:

| Phase | Task | Suggested Duration |
|---|---|---|
| First | Setting up API keys and testing video generation services and technologies | 10 days |
| Second | Experimenting with the generation of scenes and characters, and verifying the quality of the results | 8 days |
| Third | Developing a mechanism for content analysis and converting information into a narrative script | 8 days |
| Fourth | Handling file ingestion, processing, and preparing academic content | 6 days |
| Fifth | Developing a mechanism for creating assessments and evaluating student responses | 6 days |
| Sixth | Integrating the implemented components and testing the end-to-end workflow | 7 days |
| Seventh | Addressing errors, making improvements, and preparing final documentation | 5 days |
| **Total** | | **50 days** |

Note: This table is a preliminary proposal for time management; it is neither a final execution plan nor a schedule approved by the supervisor. It may be adjusted based on the results of technical experiments, any challenges that arise during the work, and guidance provided by the supervisor.



## Final result

By studying the SMART framework and applying its criteria to the project’s objectives, it became clear that this approach helps organize work and transform a general concept into specific, actionable goals that can be monitored and evaluated.
Furthermore, applying these criteria helped identify aspects requiring early technical testing—particularly video generation—clarified the connection between individual objectives and the primary goal, and facilitated the creation of an initial timeline aligned with the available timeframe.



## Conclusion

In conclusion, the study of the SMART framework has helped provide a clearer understanding of how to define project goals and organize work phases. It has also highlighted the importance of accounting for technical challenges when assigning tasks and estimating the time required for their completion, while ensuring that the plan remains flexible enough to be adjusted based on work outcomes and academic guidance.
